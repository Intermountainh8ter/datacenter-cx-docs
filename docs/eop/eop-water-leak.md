# EOP: Water Leak in a Critical Space

> Example EOP. Valve locations and isolation effects are site-specific.

## 1. Document control

| Field | Entry |

| EOP ID and revision | |
| Event covered | |
| Owner / approver / date | |
| Last drill date | |
| Posted locations | |

## 2. Entry conditions
Leak detection alarm, visible water, or a report of dripping in or above an electrical room, data hall, or plant room.

## 3. Pre-site plan (preparedness)
- [ ] Valve charts posted, showing what each isolation valve feeds and what stops when it is closed
- [ ] Isolation valves tagged, accessible, and exercised
- [ ] Spill kit staged: absorbents, plastic sheeting, wet vacuum, drain covers
- [ ] Leak detection tested on its SOP cycle
- [ ] Plumbing and mechanical vendor emergency contacts current
- [ ] Spare hoses, clamps, and common fittings in stock

## 4. Immediate actions

| # | Action | Expected response |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Keep people out of water near energized equipment | Area controlled |
| 2 | Find the source and the direction of travel | Source known |
| 3 | Protect equipment below and nearby with sheeting; divert water to a drain | Water kept off equipment |
| 4 | Notify the shift lead | Acknowledged |

## 5. Follow-up actions

| # | Action | Expected response | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Check the valve chart: what does closing the nearest valve stop? | Effect known | |
| 2 | If closing it keeps required cooling, close it | Leak stops | |
| 3 | If closing it removes required cooling, start redundant equipment first, then close it | Cooling kept, leak stops | |
| 4 | If water has reached electrical equipment, treat it as energized and hazardous. Decide de-energization with the shift lead using the one-line | Decision logged | |
| 5 | Remove water; check under the raised floor and in cable trays | Dry | |
| 6 | Check affected equipment before it is returned to service | Inspected | |

## 6. Escalation and communication

| When | Who is told | By whom |
|---|---|---|
| At once | Shift lead | Operator |
| Water on electrical equipment or loss of cooling | Facility manager, owner, vendor | Shift lead |

## 7. Do not
- Do not close a valve before checking what it feeds.
- Do not stand in water to operate electrical equipment.
- Do not re-energize wet equipment without inspection and testing.

## 8. Backout procedure (return to normal)
- **Conditions:** leak repaired and pressure tested; affected equipment inspected and dry.
- **Steps:** reopen isolation valves slowly, watching the repair; return standby equipment to its normal rotation; re-energize any isolated electrical equipment under a MOP.
- **Verification:** leak detection clear and reset, normal lineup, no alarms.

## 9. After the event
- [ ] Cause recorded; similar fittings elsewhere inspected
- [ ] Spill kit and spares restocked
- [ ] Post-event review held
