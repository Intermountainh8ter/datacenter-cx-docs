# EOP: Severe Weather Preparation and Response

> Example EOP for forecast events such as storms, high winds, flooding, extreme heat or cold, and heavy snow or ice. Adapt the triggers and actions to the site's location and design.

## 1. Document control
Use the block from the [EOP Template](eop-template.md).

## 2. Entry conditions
An official weather warning for the site area, or conditions on site that threaten utilities, access, or equipment.

## 3. Pre-site plan (preparedness)
- [ ] Site weather risks listed with the design limits of the facility for each (from the design basis)
- [ ] Trigger levels defined: watch, warning, and event, with actions for each
- [ ] Fuel supplier pre-storm delivery arrangement in place
- [ ] Staffing plan for extended shifts: who stays, rest areas, food and water
- [ ] Vendor emergency contacts current; vendors know how to reach the site if roads close
- [ ] Roof drains, storm drains, and sumps inspected on a cycle; sump pumps and alarms tested
- [ ] Outdoor equipment anchorage and loose-material checks on a cycle
- [ ] Seasonal readiness done: freeze protection, heat tracing, cooling plant readiness for heat
- [ ] Critical spares and consumables stocked before the season

## 4. Immediate actions (on a warning, before the event)

| # | Action | Expected response |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shift lead declares the weather level and starts a log | Level declared |
| 2 | Cancel or back out planned work; close open change windows | Normal lineup, full redundancy |
| 3 | Top up fuel; confirm generator readiness with a run under the SOP if time allows | Fuel at the site's storm level |
| 4 | Walk the site: secure loose items, clear drains, close doors and louvers as designed, check sumps | Site secured |
| 5 | Confirm staffing, communications, and supplies for the expected duration | Coverage confirmed |
| 6 | Notify the facility manager, owner, and monitoring center | Acknowledged |

## 5. Follow-up actions (during the event)

| # | Action | Expected response | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Increase rounds where it is safe; watch for water entry, roof and drain problems | No water entry, or leak EOP started | |
| 2 | Watch utility quality; expect transfers | Loss of utility EOP ready | |
| 3 | Extreme heat: watch heat rejection plant, condenser and cooling tower conditions, and room temperatures | Within limits, or loss of cooling EOP started | |
| 4 | Extreme cold: watch freeze protection, heat tracing, fuel temperature, and intake louvers | Normal | |
| 5 | Keep people indoors during lightning and high wind; no roof or yard work | People safe | |
| 6 | Log fuel use and recalculate run time if on generator | Recorded | |

## 6. Escalation and communication

| When | Who is told | By whom |
|---|---|---|
| On a warning | Facility manager, owner, monitoring center | Shift lead |
| Site access lost, flooding, or structural damage | Owner, emergency services as needed | Facility manager |

## 7. Do not
- Do not send people outside or onto roofs during the event.
- Do not start planned work until the all-clear.
- Do not drive through flood water to reach or leave the site.

## 8. Backout procedure (return to normal)
- **Conditions:** official warning lifted; utility stable; site walked and safe.
- **Steps:** inspect roofs, drains, outdoor equipment, and fuel systems for damage; return any equipment placed in a storm configuration to normal, one system at a time; refill fuel; stand down extra staffing; reopen the change calendar.
- **Verification:** normal lineup, full redundancy, no water in critical spaces, alarms clear.

## 9. After the event
- [ ] Damage recorded with photographs; repairs ticketed
- [ ] Consumables and spares restocked
- [ ] Post-event review held; trigger levels and actions adjusted
